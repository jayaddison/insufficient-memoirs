I'm James, a software developer from Edinburgh.

Born September 1984 in the Western General Hospital in Edinburgh, to Paul Addison (a history professor at the University of Edinburgh) and Rosemary Addison (an artist and publisher, with experience in the mapmaking industry at Bartholomews publishers).

At the time, we lived in a ground-and-basement flat on Buckingham Terrace near the Dean Village, and my grandmother on my mother's side lived at a top-floor flat at the opposite end of the street, overlooking Orchard Brae and the Forth.

My proudest achievement is bicycling across the United States of America in 2018 with my partner at the time, Kate.  The idea originated from a film screening of 'Inspired to Ride' (featuring the now-late British cyclist Mike Hall) that she and I attended in Oakland, California at The Grand Lake theatre.  A digital archivist, she wrote a journal of the adventure and published it on the world-wide-web at: https://medium.com/@jkcycling

We set off from San Francisco, and arrived in Washington D.C. some 89 days or so later, with plenty of sightseeing, variable weather conditions, brewery visits, and conversations with other cyclists, travelers and locals along the way.  We mostly followed routes mapped out by the Adventure Cycling Association, and followed their bikecentennial route 76 for much of the journey.

Both before and afterward the journey, I worked at Lyft, a technology startup in San Francisco.  The company was the third (after previous employers Meltwater and Iteris) to sponsor my H1B work visa, and they also sponsored my green card application.  Obtaining the green card allowed me to be unemployed for more than a few days, a situation that was not legally allowed under my work visa, and that enabled me to do the bicycle ride.

I think that ever since the bicycle ride I have found it difficult to readjust to regular employment.

I've been very fortunate in life, and very lucky - despite sometimes being very ignorant, oblivious, or making significant mistakes.

The earliest of my emails that I have access to at the moment is a pizza delivery order receipt from Papa John's on Leith Walk.

Travel to Ipswich

- On my return to the United Kingdom after a visit to Europe in 2024 with my bicycle, I forgot to bring my messenger bag with me on a ferry - I had left it on a bench on a pier near Harwich; most of my other belongings (including essentials such as my passport) were in my panniers and the bag had slipped my mind.
- It would have taken a relatively long time to take the ferry back and then back again to continue onwards.  I was not sure where I had left the bag at that point, and phoned a cafe in case that's where I'd left it.
- I decided to continue onwards and hope that it would be found.  I was with another cyclist; she checked I was doing OK and had the essentials I needed.
- At my hotel that night I phoned my partner and let her know what had happened.  I was also contacted by the local port authority at some point around this time - maybe that day or maybe the next day, when I met my partner in Cambridge.  Certainly I remember stopping during the cycle towards Cambridge to receive a phone call from a police officer who had my bag with them.
- I decided not to collect the bag while I was on the bicycle tour; instead I continued on with my partner to EMFcamp, and then we returned to Edinburgh, where I planned a journey south to collect it.
- The nearest hub train station was Ipswich, so I stayed there briefly on my way to Clacton to collect the bag (a less-than-24-hour round-trip).
- I witnessed the recent aftermath of a stabbing, and later reported what I had experienced to the local police after I was safely home in Edinburgh.
- I was shaken by the event; it likely did not help that I was already nervous about the journey because of the logistics and timing.
